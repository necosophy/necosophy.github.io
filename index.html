necosophy

generative interspecies assemblages

Human, plant, machine — collaborating without a center.

Rather than restoring balance, I'm interested in how we exist within instability, tension, and continuous transformation — thinking and living without fantasies of purity, permanence, or total control.

Each tool here treats technology as a sensory organ — a way of listening to forms of life that sit outside human perception, and asking what it means to collaborate with them artistically. This is translation, not representation: not an attempt to depict life as it is, but to give it another form to move through.

Nothing here assumes nature and technology are separate, or that collaboration needs a center — plants, microbes, and machines organize without a plan, a kind of cooperation capitalism has little use for. Working the boundary between organism and instrument is one way of asking what widens empathy: perception decides what counts as alive enough to matter, and most life sits past its edge.
